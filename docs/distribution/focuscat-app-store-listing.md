### Description

Focus Cat sits beside your timer and taps along while you work. Start a pomodoro, block distractions, and let a small cat keep you company through each session.

If you've tried complex productivity systems and found them exhausting, Focus Cat is a calmer place to start.

**Starting is the hardest part.** Focus Cat makes it one tap. Choose Pomodoro or countdown, set an intention if you'd like, and begin. Your cat taps along quietly from the first session. Tap it and it meows back.

**You already know what distracts you.** Create Focus Profiles to block those apps and websites when you need to. A profile for deep work. A profile for studying. One tap to switch. Schedule them to activate automatically, so your focus is ready before you are.

**Where did the time go?** Focus Cat keeps track so you don't have to. Browse past sessions, see which apps you used, spot patterns. A gentle way to reflect on how you work—not judge it.

**Progress without pressure.** Set a daily focus goal if you'd like. A quiet progress bar shows how you're doing. No streaks, no penalties. Skip a day. Life comes first.

**Your cat grows with you.** Choose from different faces and hats. Shuffle for a surprise. Gentle sounds keep the atmosphere cozy—timer ticks, a chime when you finish, and the occasional meow.

**A timer, not a system.** Focus Cat doesn't try to manage your tasks, your calendar, or your habits. A clean timer, a way to block distractions, and a cat that stays by your side. That's all it needs to be.

**Privacy first.** Activity tracking stays entirely on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere. You choose what gets tracked—apps, websites, or nothing at all. Focus Cat is open source, so you can verify this yourself.

Focus Cat is just there to sit with you while you work.

## Target Audience

### Primary: Productivity tool refugees

People who've tried Notion, Todoist, Forest, complex habit trackers—and burned out. They don't need another system. They need something simple that doesn't make them feel bad when they skip a day. They value calm over gamification, and they're drawn to tools that respect their time instead of demanding more of it.

### Secondary: Students and remote workers

Students studying for exams and remote workers/freelancers who lack external structure. They get pulled into distracting apps and websites, and they know it. They want gentle guardrails—not a productivity coach. The cat companion resonates because working alone can feel isolating, and a small presence on screen makes focus feel less lonely.

### Tertiary: Cozy app enthusiasts

People drawn to aesthetic, gentle software—the kind who use apps like Flora, Forest, or Bongo Cat. They care about how a tool feels, not just what it does. The cat, the sounds, the visual design matter as much as the timer itself.

### Shared traits

- **Age**: 18–35
- **Platform**: macOS users (often students, creatives, developers, remote workers)
- **Values**: Simplicity over features. Privacy over cloud sync. Calm over urgency.
- **Pain points**: Distraction, procrastination, loneliness while working alone, overwhelm from complex tools
- **What they search for**: "pomodoro timer mac", "focus app", "block distracting websites", "study timer", "productivity app mac"
